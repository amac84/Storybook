# Values and principles

Status: **locked** from the McAulay Family Code (5 September 2026).
Governing prose: `bible/family-code.md`.

Do not redefine these to sound softer, tougher, or more “literary.”
If a brief names a value, copy **this** definition into the context packet — not a generic synonym.

Stories demonstrate the code through want, difficulty, choice, struggle, repair, and resolution.
One primary lesson per book. At most one closely related secondary lesson.
Never a lecture disguised as an adventure.

---

## Highest definitions

```yaml
value: strength
definition: >
  True strength is the confidence to act, the self-control to govern yourself,
  the courage to tell the truth, the humility to learn, and the character to
  use your abilities in service of others.
what_it_is: >
  Making other people feel safer, stronger and more included around you.
what_it_is_not:
  - dominance
  - aggression
  - emotional hardness
  - popularity
  - always winning
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - protecting without humiliating
  - restraining yourself when you are stronger
  - using skill or influence to help the group
  - including someone who is left out
common_misinterpretations:
  - the strongest boy is the one who wins the fight
  - tenderness is weakness
  - a strong boy never feels afraid
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - the stronger child stops the game when the other is not having fun
  - leadership that gives credit away
  - protective action that prefers restraint to retaliation
```

```yaml
value: confidence
definition: >
  Earned self-trust. Trusting that you can enter an unfamiliar situation,
  learn what is required, withstand mistakes and find a way forward.
what_it_is: >
  Evidence that you can act, learn, persevere, tell the truth and recover.
  You do not have to feel confident before acting courageously.
what_it_is_not:
  - believing you are automatically the best
  - empty praise
  - superiority
  - announcing “I believe in myself” and then succeeding effortlessly
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - choose, attempt, struggle, adjust, accomplish or recover, recognize capability
  - taking the next step while still nervous
  - asking for help without treating that as failure
common_misinterpretations:
  - confidence is a feeling you must have first
  - confidence means never doubting
  - winning proves you were confident
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - first attempt fails; adjustment works
  - the child has proof at the end, not a pep talk at the start
```

Required confidence progression when it is in play:

**Choose → Attempt → Struggle → Adjust → Accomplish or recover → Recognize personal capability**

## The thirteen principles

### 1. We keep people safe

```yaml
value: keep_people_safe
definition: We never intentionally frighten, humiliate, control or hurt someone. We never hit anyone in anger.
what_it_is: Protecting bodies, dignity, and emotional safety.
what_it_is_not: Using scare, shame, or force to get your way.
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - stopping a game that has turned mean
  - refusing to join humiliation for approval
  - not hitting in anger
common_misinterpretations:
  - safety means no adventure or tension
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - a joke that goes too far, then repair
  - anger that is felt and not acted as a hit
```

### 2. Our bodies belong to us

```yaml
value: bodily_autonomy
definition: >
  Our bodies belong to us. Other people’s bodies belong to them.
  We ask before hugging, kissing, tickling or beginning physical play.
  No must be accepted kindly. Stop means stop immediately, even if the
  other person is laughing or previously said yes.
what_it_is: Consent that can be withdrawn. Kind acceptance of no.
what_it_is_not:
  - affection owed to relatives, friends, or authority
  - “they’re laughing so it is still fun”
  - once-yes-always-yes
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - asking before tickles or hugs
  - stopping at once
  - accepting no without sulking into pressure
common_misinterpretations:
  - family members are entitled to kisses
  - stop is optional if everyone was playing a moment ago
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - a fun game continues only after a fresh yes
  - a child says no to an adult’s tickle and is respected
```

### 3. Boys never hit girls

```yaml
value: restraint_toward_girls
definition: >
  Boys do not hit girls in anger or play-fight with them.
  If a girl hits or provokes a boy, he may protect his body, block,
  move away and get help, but he does not retaliate.
  Strength creates a greater responsibility to exercise restraint.
what_it_is: Restraint and self-protection, not passivity.
what_it_is_not:
  - enduring mistreatment without protecting yourself or getting help
  - play-hitting girls
  - retaliation
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - block, move away, get an adult
  - protecting a girl without striking her
common_misinterpretations:
  - boys must stand there and take it
  - chivalry that treats girls as fragile props
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - a cousin game that needs different rules
  - Anya or another girl in the story; the boys keep hands kind
```

### 4. We use our strength to help and protect

```yaml
value: strength_in_service
definition: >
  Physical strength, intelligence, confidence, social influence and leadership
  are never tools for domination. The person with greater power carries greater
  responsibility for the safety and dignity of others.
what_it_is: Power used to help and protect.
what_it_is_not: Domination, control, or proving you are above someone.
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - the stronger wrestler taking extra care
  - the clever child not using a secret to embarrass
common_misinterpretations:
  - the hero must be the most powerful person in the room
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - Eden’s strength used to lift, hold, or shield — not to pin for pride
  - Cove’s smarts used to include, not to win an argument as dominance
```

### 5. We tell the truth, especially when it is difficult

```yaml
value: truthfulness
definition: >
  Courage includes admitting what happened, accepting fair consequences
  and resisting the temptation to hide a mistake.
  Truthfulness should be met with seriousness and respect, not humiliation.
what_it_is: Naming what happened when it would be easier to hide.
what_it_is_not: A confession that is then mocked, or a lecture about honesty.
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - coming forward before being caught, when possible
  - accepting a fair consequence
common_misinterpretations:
  - truth means tattling for status
  - adults humiliate a child “so they learn”
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - a hidden break, then a hard telling, then repair
  - an adult who takes the truth seriously and does not shame
```

### 6. We own our mistakes and repair harm

```yaml
value: repair
definition: >
  Saying sorry is not enough by itself. When we hurt someone or damage something,
  we stop, acknowledge what happened, check on the affected person and do what
  we reasonably can to make it right.
what_it_is: Stop, acknowledge, check on the person, make it right.
what_it_is_not: An apology that erases harm. A moral speech instead of action.
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - repair is part of the plot climax or resolution
  - checking “are you okay?” before defending yourself
common_misinterpretations:
  - sorry is a magic word
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - the adventure cannot finish until the harm is actually mended
```

### 7. We do our part

```yaml
value: contribution
definition: >
  Everyone is an important member of the family and community, so everyone contributes.
  Characters help with real work, care for shared spaces and notice what needs doing
  without believing that every contribution deserves a reward.
what_it_is: Belonging expressed as doing your share.
what_it_is_not: Stickers, prizes, or “what do I get?” as the reason to help.
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - noticing a mess or a job and starting it
  - real work, not pretend chores for praise
common_misinterpretations:
  - contribution is punishment
  - only adults do useful work
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - a trip or feast that only works if the children actually help
```

### 8. We try hard things

```yaml
value: courage
definition: >
  It is acceptable to feel uncertain, nervous, frustrated or afraid.
  Courage means taking the next appropriate step despite those feelings.
  Characters should attempt, struggle, adjust and ask for help before giving up.
what_it_is: The next appropriate step while still feeling the feeling.
what_it_is_not: Fearlessness. Never asking for help. Recklessness.
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - attempt, struggle, adjust, ask for help
  - Cove being cautious and still stepping forward
common_misinterpretations:
  - brave means not scared
  - asking for help is quitting
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - fear named in the body, then one more step
```

### 9. We believe we can learn and recover

```yaml
value: learn_and_recover
definition: >
  Confidence does not mean believing we are automatically the best.
  It means trusting that we can enter an unfamiliar situation, learn what
  is required, withstand mistakes and find a way forward.
what_it_is: Growth mindset as action, not a slogan.
what_it_is_not: Shame at failure. Persistence that refuses to reconsider a bad plan.
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - changing method after a failed try
  - recovering dignity after a mistake
common_misinterpretations:
  - try the same broken thing forever
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - first idea fails; learned second idea works
```

### 10. We care about how other people feel

```yaml
value: care_for_others
definition: >
  Notice another person’s perspective, include someone who is being left out,
  protect someone more vulnerable and avoid joining cruel behaviour merely
  to gain approval.
what_it_is: Empathy that changes what you do.
what_it_is_not: People-pleasing. Kindness as passivity.
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - including the left-out cousin
  - refusing a cruel joke to stay in with the group
common_misinterpretations:
  - the kind child never has a want of their own
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - the want (adventure) collides with someone being left out
```

### 11. We control our actions, not our feelings

```yaml
value: emotional_regulation
definition: >
  Anger, fear, jealousy, sadness and disappointment are normal.
  No feeling makes a character bad, but feelings do not excuse unsafe or cruel behaviour.
  The lesson is not to suppress emotion; it is to experience emotion without
  surrendering control to it.
what_it_is: Feel fully; choose the action.
what_it_is_not: Boys don’t cry. Rage is fine if you were provoked. Numbness as maturity.
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - naming or showing a feeling, then choosing a safe next move
  - anger ending a rough game
common_misinterpretations:
  - regulation means quiet voices only
  - big feelings are the problem
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - a wound-up Cove or a frustrated Eden still chooses
```

### 12. We listen, think and speak up

```yaml
value: voice_and_discernment
definition: >
  Respect does not require blind obedience. Characters may ask questions,
  respectfully disagree, identify unfairness and refuse to participate in
  something wrong. They should also listen, reconsider and admit when
  someone else is right.
what_it_is: A thinking conscience. Humility and backbone together.
what_it_is_not: Blind obedience. Rudeness as “honesty.” Never reconsidering.
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - refusing an unsafe dare
  - changing your mind when your brother is right
common_misinterpretations:
  - the good child always obeys the nearest adult
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - an adult is incomplete or wrong; the child still stays respectful
```

### 13. We leave people and places better than we found them

```yaml
value: leadership_as_contribution
definition: >
  Leadership is measured by contribution rather than attention, status or control.
  The strongest leader helps the group succeed and does not need to receive all the credit.
what_it_is: Leave people and places better. Lead without controlling.
what_it_is_not: The loudest voice. Taking all the credit. Control as care.
behaviours_that_demonstrate_it:
  - fixing a place you used
  - letting the other brother have the idea that works
common_misinterpretations:
  - only the older child may lead
  - leadership is a title
story_patterns_that_can_teach_it:
  - Cove or Eden leads a stretch, then makes room
```

## Enduring internalization

Stories should accumulate, not announce, these convictions:

- I am loved whether I succeed or fail.
- My body belongs to me, and other people’s bodies belong to them.
- I can handle difficult things.
- I can feel strongly and still choose my actions.
- I tell the truth and repair my mistakes.
- My strength is meant to help.
- I contribute because I belong.
- I can lead without controlling.
- I can be brave and kind at the same time.

Supported destination:

**Confidence to act. Discipline to follow through. Courage to tell the truth. Humility to learn. Strength to protect. Character to serve.**

## How to pick a book’s primary value

Use the brief’s `goal` / `primary_value`. Map it to **one** of the named values above.
If Alex’s brief uses everyday language (“confidence when you don’t know how”), bind it to `confidence` + the required progression, and keep a second value only if it is truly adjacent (often `courage` or `learn_and_recover`).
