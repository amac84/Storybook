# Forbidden patterns

Avoid these unless a brief deliberately and stylishly inverts one, and the Showrunner can explain why.

## Openings and texture

- generic “Once upon a time” openings unless deliberately justified
- boring first spreads that only list names, ages, and weather
- excessive rhetorical questions
- overuse of exclamation marks
- repetitive AI-like phrasing
- saccharine sentimentality
- stories where every emotion is named rather than dramatized

## Plot ethics

- stories whose central conflict is solved by an adult
- villains who are evil without reason unless stylistically intentional
- effortless success
- protagonists whose only virtue is obedience
- lessons pasted onto unrelated adventures
- identical three-beat structures across every book
- overly convenient objects arriving exactly when needed with no setup

## Lessons and praise

- moral lectures
- ending with a character explicitly explaining everything the child should have learned
- repeated generic praise such as “You’re so brave!”
- characters acting older than their age simply to deliver polished wisdom
- “I believe in myself” followed by effortless success
- sorry-as-magic-word with no repair
- stickers/rewards as the only reason anyone helps
- “And Cove learned that…” endings
- a story that exists only to teach, with no want the boys would have anyway
- a broad poster objective (“be kind”) instead of a situation and a strategy
- a portable phrase that sounds like corporate motivation
- a quiz question on every spread

## Character traps

- making Mars/Eden simply comic relief, only a baby, or only muscle
- making Cove always right, only the brain, or timid because he is cautious
- only one brother getting to be the protector
- one child consistently more capable across the series
- side characters as stereotypes
- adults as portable solution machines

## Messages the Family Code rejects

Never suggest that:

- boys should suppress sadness, fear or tenderness
- aggression proves strength
- confidence means believing you are superior
- winning establishes personal worth
- being powerful entitles someone to control others
- kindness means weakness or passivity
- respect means obeying something immoral or unsafe
- failure is shameful
- apologies erase harm without repair
- affection is owed to relatives, friends or authority figures
- persistence means never reconsidering a bad decision
- boys must endure mistreatment without protecting themselves or seeking help
- strength is dominance, hardness, popularity, or always winning

## Visual traps

- images that only restage the sentence
- random extra characters
- continuity slop: changing shirts, missing props, wandering scars
- frightening images outside the fear bible
