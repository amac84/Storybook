# Story philosophy

Status: aligned to the Family Code and the Story Design Principles.
Do not copy the nine-beat rhythm as an identical plot every time.
One precise objective per book. Story first. Protect the magic.

## Picture books, not chapters

A book is a sequence of illustrated spreads. Each spread has a job: hook, desire, discovery, worsening, choice, climax, or release. The last line of a spread often creates a page-turn.

## Desire before plot furniture

A child should know what someone wants before the scenery becomes impressive. Adventure is the pressure on that desire, not a tour of locations.

## Agency

Whenever reasonable, Cove and/or Mars meaningfully affect the resolution. Adults may help, warn, or fail to see. They do not arrive as the solution.

## Lesson through plot

The intended principle should shape:

- the character’s choices
- the problem
- the escalation
- the climax
- the resolution

Prefer demonstration over explanation. If the last spread can be deleted and the lesson disappears, the lesson was a speech.

The child must experience the lesson before it is explained.

Start from a want, not from “Cove needs to learn X.”

Carry one portable phrase. Repeat the idea through see → try → own, not through explanation.

Keep stakes child-sized even when the adventure is large. Parents are competent and human — not fools, not rescuers.

Repair, when needed, is part of the action — not an appended apology.

Courage and kindness may cost something real.

Characters may be angry, afraid, jealous, impulsive, dishonest, or discouraged. The code governs the response and the repair, not a demand for flawless behaviour.

Confidence, when in play, follows: Choose → Attempt → Struggle → Adjust → Accomplish or recover → Recognize personal capability.

## Difficulty

The problem should get worse, or more complicated, after the first attempt. Effortless competence is forbidden unless the joke is that competence does not solve *this* problem.

## Feeling

Warmth is not the same as sweetness. Fear must stay inside `bible/fear-and-content-boundaries.md`. Relief should be earned.

## Humour

Humour is a structural ingredient: character mismatch, surprise, visual asides, repeated patterns with variation. It is not a garnish of puns sprinkled on a lecture.

## Reread value

A first read should pull forward. A reread should reward noticing. Leave some information in the pictures. Pay off setups.

## Variety

Do not force every engagement principle into every book. Do not reuse the same three-beat shape, the same solver, or the same location type book after book.

## Originality over score-chasing

Editorial scores help the Showrunner see weak areas. They are not a rubric to game. A slightly rougher, more itself story beats a mechanically “complete” one.
