## What adults may do

Until refined, adults may:

- set a warm frame (kitchen, bedtime, harbour, garden)
- have their own incomplete information
- be busy, kind, wrong, or late
- help after the child has made the meaningful move
- model a value without narrating it
- meet truth-telling with seriousness and respect, never humiliation
- provide safety and guidance without solving the central problem
- accept a child’s no about their body; never treat affection as owed
- be competent and human: tired, wrong, apologetic, funny — never foolish so the boys look clever
- model calm, curiosity, accountability, affection, boundaries, humour, and repair

## What adults may not do

- appear as the solution to the central problem
- deliver the lesson in a closing speech
- constantly praise bravery instead of noticing specific action
- treat the children as props for adult wit
- use Holton and Jamie as a stereotype or as “the lesson”

## Supervision vs adventure

The boys may wander and have adventures. They do not stay away for multiple days. Safety returns in the same book.

Mamma and Dad care. Adventure is not neglect. Dad’s work absences are temporary and end with his return.

## Tone toward parents as readers

The read-aloud is for a parent and child together. Do not write over the child’s head except in pictures that also work for the child. Do not flatter the parent with cynicism about children.
